Note/summary/flow-chart completionShort answer
Paper vs. computer-delivered. On the paper-based test you get an extra 10 minutes at the end to transfer your answers onto the answer sheet in pencil. On the computer-delivered test there's no separate transfer step — you type answers directly as you listen and get about 2 minutes at the end to review them on-screen. Total time is close to 30 minutes either way; spelling and word limits (e.g. “NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS”) are graded identically in both formats.
  1. 1Read ahead, every time. You get a short pause before each section to preview the questions — use every second of it. Knowing what's coming is worth more than any listening skill.
  2. 2Answers come in order. The information for Q1 always appears before the information for Q2 in the recording — if you're stuck, don't stop, move on and catch up at the next obvious marker.
  3. 3Distractors are deliberate. Speakers often say something and then correct themselves (“Tuesday — actually, sorry, make that Wednesday”). The final, corrected answer is the one that counts.
  4. 4Spelling counts. Correctly-spelled common words and correctly copied names/numbers (from the recording, spelled out or written on screen) are required for the mark — check word limits carefully.
