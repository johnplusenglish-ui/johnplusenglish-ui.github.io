The big story

Scores just hit their lowest point on record

Across the OECD, 2025 recorded the weakest average performance PISA has ever measured in science, reading and maths. Reading has slid since it peaked around 2012, science has drifted down for a decade, and maths fell sharply after 2018. One striking clue: the share of "hasty readers" who rush through a text and give quick but wrong answers has almost doubled since 2018, exactly the careful-reading skill that matters most in the age of AI.

Money is not the answer

Spending more does not buy better results

Luxembourg spends by far the most per student, yet scores below three countries that invest less than half as much. How money is used matters more than how much there is.

Worth a mention

Findings to bring to class

Beyond the scores, PISA 2025 asked students how they learn, feel and use technology.

57%

Learning is losing its shine

Just over half of students say learning new things is not boring, but that share fell in 60 systems since 2022. No system improved.

28%

Phones are a classroom problem

More than a quarter say classmates are distracted by devices in most science lessons. In China, Japan and Korea it is under 10%. Clear phone rules help.

90%+

Green careers appeal

Over nine in ten students in Viet Nam and Indonesia want a job that helps the environment, against 62% on average, and girls lead the way.

AI

More AI is not more learning

Students who lean on AI for specific schoolwork tend to score lower, not higher. The gains show up only when AI use comes with real AI-literacy skills.

39

Belonging is bouncing back

In 39 systems more students feel they belong at school than in 2022, and fewer feel lonely in 48 systems. Discipline improved most in Korea and Croatia.

3¼

Three in four reach the baseline

About three-quarters of students can use basic scientific evidence to explain everyday phenomena, the level PISA calls proficient.
