Cold open

Right, so picture Vienna, 1907. A brilliant, difficult Swiss psychiatrist turns up at the home of a Jewish doctor twenty years his senior, a man half the medical establishment already thinks is a crank. They start talking after lunch. They do not stop for thirteen hours.

Six years later they will not be speaking at all, and one of them will spend the next four years having visions of Europe drowning in blood, writing them into a book bound in red leather that his own family will not dare publish for almost a century.

After 1961

Why you already know more Jung than you think

He never won the mainstream over the way Freud did. He won something stranger: he leaked into the culture at large until his vocabulary stopped sounding like his.

The Hero's Journey

Joseph Campbell built The Hero with a Thousand Faces on Jungian archetypes. George Lucas built Star Wars on Campbell. Luke Skywalker's whole arc, mentor, threshold, shadow, return, is Jung's individuation myth wearing a poncho.

Your everyday vocabulary

"Introvert," "extrovert," "complex," "persona," "synchronicity." All Jung's coinages, or his repurposings, now so ordinary nobody credits him. That is a strange kind of immortality: total absorption.

The personality test industry

Myers-Briggs is Jung's typology filtered through a mother-daughter team who never trained as psychologists. Jung never endorsed it and likely would have hated the certainty it sells. It is now used in job interviews worldwide anyway.

Twelve steps

Jung corresponded with Bill Wilson, co-founder of Alcoholics Anonymous, about a former patient. His letter, arguing addiction was a thwarted spiritual hunger ("spiritus contra spiritum"), is quoted in AA literature to this day.

The scientific verdict

Most of his big claims, the collective unconscious, archetypes as inherited structures, are considered unfalsifiable by mainstream psychology and sit closer to philosophy or comparative mythology than clinical science.

The unresolved question

His 1933–39 leadership of the International General Medical Society for Psychotherapy under Nazi rule, and a 1934 essay contrasting "Jewish" and "Aryan" psychology, remain genuinely contested among historians. See Lecture Eight.

So was he a genius or a mystic who got lucky?

Both, probably, and Jung would have found the question itself a bit beside the point. He never much cared whether the unconscious was "real" in a laboratory sense. He cared whether taking it seriously made people's lives more bearable. Judged as a scientist, his record is patchy and, in places, indefensible. Judged as a myth-maker who handed the twentieth century a language for its inner life, he is one of the most influential people you have never quite finished reading.

He died on the 6th of June 1961 at his home in Küsnacht, on the lake outside Zurich, eighty-five years old, having spent the previous four decades building a tower by hand, carving stones with alchemical inscriptions, and refusing, more or less until the end, to explain himself to anyone who hadn't done the reading.
